## Source

Safe August surf alternatives (low/no US State Dept advisory), aggregated from State Dept advisories, Surf Atlas, Lapoint, Stormrider, Big Wave Dave, Sayulita Life, and flight aggregators, fetched 2026-06-02. Context: the group wanted options *without* Nicaragua's **Level 3 "Reconsider Travel."**

### US State Dept advisory levels (verified this session)
- 🇭🇳 **Nicaragua — Level 3** "Reconsider Travel" (the one to avoid).
- 🇨🇷 **Costa Rica — Level 2** "Exercise Increased Caution" (reference baseline).
- 🇸🇻 **El Salvador — Level 1** "Exercise Normal Precautions" — upgraded after a major security turnaround; now one of the safest in the region.
- 🇲🇽 **Mexico — state-specific.** Surf areas of interest: **Nayarit (Sayulita/San Pancho) = Level 2**; **Baja California Sur (Los Cabos/Todos Santos) = Level 2** (low homicide rates). Some other Mexican states are Level 3–4 — not these.
- 🏝️ **Hawaii — domestic US, no advisory at all.**

### 🇸🇻 El Salvador (El Sunzal / El Tunco, La Libertad)
- **August = peak wet-season swell** — consistent head-high+, regularly 8–12 ft on the points; favorable winds. Warm water, boardshorts.
- **El Sunzal** = long, mellow right point — "the most forgiving learning wave in Central America"; **3–5-day progression realistic for first-timers.** **El Tunco** = lively surf town, beginner lessons; **La Paz** (inside section at Punta Roca) for beginners. (Outer points get powerful in peak season — beginners stay on the inside.)
- **Flights:** nonstop to **San Salvador (SAL)** from Miami, Houston, Dallas, LA, Atlanta (AA/United/Delta/Avianca/Volaris); from NYC/SF = **1 stop**, cheap (~$350–500 RT).
- **Transfer:** SAL → El Tunco/El Sunzal **~45 min / 25 mi** (El Sunzal ~20 min) — Uber ~$25–35 or private ~$30–40. *Much* shorter than Nicaragua.
- **Camps:** Lapoint El Sunzal (premium), 360 Surf Camp El Sunzal, Laola Surfcamp, El Salvador Surf Camps, plus El Tunco surf schools.

### 🇲🇽 Mexico — Riviera Nayarit (Sayulita / San Pancho)
- **Level 2.** Beginner-friendly mellow beach break in a lively town; warm water. Fly **Puerto Vallarta (PVR)** (many US nonstops) + ~45 min drive N to Sayulita.
- **August caveat:** rainy/hurricane season (Jun–Nov tropical-storm risk); surf still works. Petty theft the main concern; tourist areas safe.

### 🇲🇽 Mexico — Baja California Sur (Todos Santos / Cerritos)
- **Level 2**, consistently low homicide. **Playa Cerritos** = beginner-friendly beach break; warm. Fly **Los Cabos (SJD)** (many US nonstops incl. SFO) + ~1 h to Cerritos/Todos Santos.

### 🏝️ Hawaii — Oahu (Waikiki)
- **No advisory (domestic).** South-shore season May–Sep; **August south swell 2–5 ft (Waikiki often 1–3 ft)**, water ~80 °F (27 °C). **Canoes & Queens** (Waikiki) = gentle sandy-bottom reef, long slow rides, hundreds of surf-school students — textbook beginner.
- **Flights:** nonstop from **SFO** (~5–6 h); from NYC nonstop or 1 stop (~10–11 h). **On-ground ~$600–1,200/wk** (board rental $30–50/day; hotels from ~$145/night). The pricier-but-zero-hassle option.

## Highlights

> El Salvador now **Level 1**: "exercise normal precautions... gang activity having decreased over the last three years."

> El Sunzal: "the most forgiving learning wave in Central America"; El Sunzal ~20 min from the airport.

> Waikiki August: south swell 2–5 ft, water ~80 °F, Canoes & Queens "break gently over a sandy-bottom reef... long and slow."

## My notes

The direct answer to "safe alternative to Nicaragua": **El Salvador (Level 1)** — same warm, cheap, peak-August Central American surf, *safer* than Costa Rica on paper, and a 45-min transfer. **Hawaii** is the zero-risk, beginner-perfect (pricier) pick; **Mexico (Nayarit/Baja Sur, Level 2)** is the easy-flights warm option.
