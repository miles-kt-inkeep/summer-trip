## Overview

**Surf week in El Salvador (El Sunzal / El Tunco) — the warm, cheap, *safe* Central American option.** This is the direct answer to "like Nicaragua/Costa Rica, but without the travel advisory": El Salvador is now **US State Dept Level 1 "Exercise Normal Precautions"** — safer on paper than Costa Rica (Level 2) after its security turnaround. You get peak August swell, boardshorts water, low costs, a **45-minute airport transfer**, and **El Sunzal — "the most forgiving learning wave in Central America,"** where 3–5 days of real progress is realistic for improvers. Full context: [safe surf alternatives](../research/safe-surf-alternatives.md).

> [!NOTE]
> **Safety:** El Salvador sits at **Level 1** as of 2026 (gang activity down sharply over three years; surf coast La Libertad has added lighting/patrols). Re-check the [current advisory](../external-sources/safe-surf-alternatives.md) before booking — levels change.

## Itinerary

A 7-night shape based at **El Tunco / El Sunzal** (La Libertad coast):

| Day | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| **Sat** | Fly NYC/SF → **San Salvador (SAL)** (1 stop via Miami/Houston/Dallas/Atlanta). **45-min** transfer to El Tunco/El Sunzal. |
| **Sun** | Settle in, board rental, warm-up at **El Sunzal** (long mellow right point). |
| **Mon–Fri** | Surf El Sunzal mornings; 1–2 coached/private sessions; El Tunco town, La Bocana, day trip to El Zonte. Stay on the **inside** when the points jack up on big swell. |
| **Sat** | Final surf; transfer to SAL. |
| **Sun** | Fly home. |

## Getting there (mid-Aug example)

> [!NOTE]
> No nonstop from NYC/SF, but easy 1-stops: **NYC →** Miami (AA) or Houston (United) → SAL. **SF →** Houston (United) or LA → SAL. Then a **45-min** Uber/private transfer ($25–40) to the coast — the shortest airport-to-surf hop of any Central American option. Camps to compare: **Lapoint El Sunzal**, **360 Surf Camp El Sunzal**, **Laola Surfcamp**, plus El Tunco surf schools.

## Bottom line

El Salvador gives you the **Nicaragua upside** (warm, cheap, consistent, uncrowded, peak August) **without the Level 3 advisory** — plus the shortest transfer and a famously gentle learning wave at El Sunzal. The one caveat: it's a **point-break coast** that gets powerful at peak swell, so keep beginners on the inside sections. Compare with [Costa Rica](./surf-costa-rica-nosara.md) (Level 2, nonstop, punchier beach breaks) and the rest of the [safe options](../research/safe-surf-alternatives.md).
